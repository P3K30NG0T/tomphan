Renting out AI GPUs: a boom with a clock on it

What the model has to answer

Value of one GPU = rent it earns − what it costs, over its useful life

Step 2: run the numbers →
Method and sources

The research agent downloads the GetDeploying GPU price dataset (CC BY 4.0) for five NVIDIA models every week, checks it, and computes each model's latest rent (average of the last four complete weeks), its change over the year, contract-term prices and the price ladder. The ladder is a least-squares fit of log rent against years since volume availability. CoreWeave figures come from SEC filings; other figures are cited where they appear.

    Recommendation

    The right move depends on who you are

    There is no single client here, so the advice is split by the position a reader might be in. It follows the assumptions above: change them and it updates.

    How the model works
    1. Rent: the year-1 rate holds while a contract locks it; after that, rent follows the market curve for a GPU of that age (year-1 rent × (1 − decline) per year).
    2. Revenue = rent × 8,760 hours × utilization. Costs = power (kW × hours × utilization × $/kWh) plus other opex per available hour.
    3. Depreciation is straight-line over the economic life; tax is 21% of EBIT, and a loss creates a tax shield used by the wider business.
    4. Free cash flow = EBITDA − tax, plus resale value in the final year. NPV discounts at the cost of capital; IRR, break-even rent and break-even capex are solved by bisection.
    5. The harness re-runs the base case in a separate Python implementation every week and blocks publishing if the two differ by more than $1.

            Design choices

            Why you can trust what it produces

            Numbers only from tools

            The agent never does arithmetic in its head. It calls a tool, and its text is checked: any number not in the evidence is rejected.

            Every figure traceable

            Each source file is stored with its size and fingerprint, so a figure on this site can be traced to the exact file it came from.

            Stop, don't guess

            If a blocking check fails, nothing is published and the site keeps the last good data. If a source is down, it says so and falls back to a recorded copy.

            People make the judgement calls

            Assumptions with no open data behind them are marked as analyst inputs and tested in the sensitivity analysis, not hidden.
